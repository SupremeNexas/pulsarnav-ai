XNAV Framework Context

X-ray Pulsar Navigation (XNAV) utilizes the highly stable spin-profiles of millisecond pulsars as cosmic beacons. By measuring the Time of Arrival (TOA) of pulses at the spacecraft and comparing them to a Solar System Barycenter (SSB) reference model, we solve for absolute state coordinates.

Key Takeaways

  • The "Observability Floor": Using 6 or more pulsars yields a sharp 10x reduction in error because it fully constrains position estimation alongside clock drift updates.
  • Noise Scaling: EKF tracking accuracy scales linearly with receiver jitter, bounded at the lower end by the pulsar's intrinsic spin-stability limit.
  • Deep Space Stability: High-period sources (like B1937+21) provide rapid convergence but require high dispersion calibration.
